Fig. 6 | Wind and multiscale energy during reintensification. a, Recorded wind speed. b, Horizontal and vertical (pressure-coordinate) contributions to canonical transfer of horizontal kinetic energy (KE), and their sum, integrated from the last wind minimum to near-peak return. Values are pressure-weighted means over 500, 600, 700 and 850 hPa, in J kg⁻¹. Positive canonical transfer denotes a gain of energy in the target scale window. Points and bars show means and percentile 95% intervals for 132 events in 122 SIDs on the common support used in Supplementary Fig. 29. c–e, Horizontal KE, dry available potential energy (APE) and same-window APE-to-KE conversion at 700 hPa. f, Net vertical KE input to the 500–850 hPa layer per unit layer mass. Panels b–f use 0–300 km means in the nominal 2–16-day window. Positive conversion supplies KE; positive layer input denotes vertical flux convergence. In a and c–f, time zero is the last wind minimum before near-peak return; lines and shading show means and pointwise 95% intervals. These composites extend beyond individual return times: at 0, 6, 12, 18 and 24 h, respectively, 123, 123, 68, 39 and 25 SIDs remain within their actual trough-to-return intervals. g, Ascent-minus-Decay dry APE at 700 hPa and 0–300 km in the >16-day, 2–16-day and <2-day windows. Dots and bars show mean differences and 95% intervals for 115 events in 108 SIDs, matched within cyclone and common 10 kt wind bins. Ascent is the interval from the last wind minimum to near-peak return. Estimates average events within SID, then weight SIDs equally; intervals use 2,000 SID bootstrap draws on each diagnostic's available sample. For the existing composites and matched comparisons, tables S9a–h and Supplementary Data 1 report endpoint changes, integrated rates, matched comparisons, sample sizes and sensitivities.

Supplementary Fig. 29 | Dry-APE canonical transfer and selected KE contributions during reintensification. a, Horizontal and vertical (pressure-coordinate) contributions to canonical transfer of dry available potential energy (APE), and their sum. b, APE-to-horizontal-kinetic-energy (KE) conversion, net vertical KE input through the 500 and 850 hPa boundaries, and total canonical KE transfer. All quantities use the nominal 2–16-day window and 0–300 km region, with common spatial and temporal support for 132 events in 122 SIDs. Rates are integrated over each actual interval from the last wind minimum to near-peak return, yielding J kg⁻¹. Layer means use trapezoidal pressure weights at 500, 600, 700 and 850 hPa; boundary input is expressed per unit layer mass. Positive canonical transfer denotes a gain in the target scale window; positive conversion and boundary input supply KE. Points and bars show equal-SID means after averaging events within SID, and percentile 95% intervals from 2,000 SID bootstrap draws. Total canonical KE transfer is repeated from Fig. 6b to compare these selected contributions on the same support.
